# AGENTS.md

Firmware for **one device: the Waveshare ESP32-S3-ePaper-3.97**, an ESP32-S3 board with a 3.97"
800×480 B/W e-paper display, PMIC + Li-ion charging, audio, RTC and sensors. Base every pin, driver
and power decision on this file, `include/board_pins.h`, or the primary sources in
[`docs/`](docs/README.md). If they disagree with the schematic, the schematic wins; fix them.

## Hardware

- **MCU:** ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 (240 MHz dual-core, 16 MB flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM, Wi-Fi + BLE 5). USB-C goes directly to the S3's native USB. No UART bridge, no reset button.
- **Display:** SSD1677 controller, 4-wire SPI, write-only. Refresh: full ≈3 s, fast ≈1.5 s, partial ≈0.3 s, 4-grey ≈3 s. Operating range 0–50 °C.
- **PMIC:** TG28, **AXP2101 register-compatible**; Waveshare uses XPowersLib's `XPowersAXP2101`.
- **Peripherals:** ES8311 codec + NS4150B speaker amp + mic, QMI8658A IMU, SHTC3, PCF85063A RTC, microSD (4-bit SDMMC), 3-way rotary switch, BOOT and PWR buttons.
- **Connectors:** MX1.25 for the 3.7 V Li-ion battery, the speaker and an RTC backup cell (marked "reserved"). Test pads on the bottom edge: 5V, GND, 3V3, RX, TX, SDA, SCL, GP0, GP1.

## Power architecture (read before writing sleep/power code)

- **Rails:** DC1 → 3V3 system rail (the ESP32, the amp's VDD, the codec's DVDD/PVDD, sensors, SD). ALDO2 → `Audio_VCC`: the codec's analog supply (AVDD, through R44) **and** the mic. **ALDO3 → the e-paper panel's power switch (Q3 → Q2); if ALDO3 is off, the panel is dead.** While on, that switch draws about 0.3 mA (Q3's base through R29, Q2's gate pull-up R20) even with the panel in deep sleep, so ALDO3 is off between updates. ALDO1 (`AudioCTR_VCC`) reaches only R74 (to the amp's CTRL), which isn't fitted, so it has no load. The chip turns on every output at power-up, including DCDC2-4 (no inductors fitted) and ALDO4/BLDO/CPUSLDO/DLDO (unconnected). `pmic::configure()` turns off all but DC1, ALDO2 and ALDO3 at every boot; `audio` switches ALDO2 per session, and the panel driver switches ALDO3 on only for an update (`Epd::show()`, `pmic.h`).
- **Fixed drain:** T1 sinks R24 (10 k from 3V3 on `PWR_KEY_SENSE`) whenever PWR isn't pressed, so about 0.33 mA flows whenever the board is on. Only a hardware change (a larger R24) helps.
- **On/off:** the PWR key drives the PMIC's `PWRON` pin (Waveshare config: 1 s press = on, 4 s = forced off). The chip's own defaults are 128 ms and 6 s (verified on hardware). Our `pmic::configure()` keeps the chip's 128 ms and sets 4 s at boot; `references/power.md` §2 has the full comparison. `axp2101.shutdown()` cuts power to everything, including the ESP32.
- **Wake from full power-off:** the RTC's INT output is diode-OR'd onto `PWRON`, so **an RTC alarm boots the board.** The intended low-power loop: update display → panel deep sleep → arm RTC alarm → PMIC shutdown. The e-paper holds its image with no power. The RTC runs from the PMIC's always-on LDO. On boot, `RTC_INT` and `PWR_KEY_SENSE` tell you why the board woke. **Our RTC holds UTC** (unlike the vendor's local time), so alarm registers are UTC; `TIMEZONE` in `.env` gives local time (`src/rtc.h`). `src/power.h` implements the loop: Sleep (wakes for alarms), standby (the Clock face, redrawn by an RTC wake every minute) Book (a book left open 15 minutes on battery: its cover, off, and the next start opens it again; `src/apps/closed_book.h`) Agent (PWR on the Agent, or while a session asks or works: the Agent face, off, and an RTC check-in every 5 to 30 minutes that syncs with inkharness, chirps and redraws only what changed; `src/apps/agent_face.h`) and Edition (PWR on News: its off face, the stories of the last 12 hours one at a time, off, and an RTC wake every minute that redraws the clock, every `news_face_every` minutes the next story, and at the end of a lap, at most hourly, fetches the feeds first; HOLD PWR opens News on the story shown; `src/apps/news_edition.h`). Sleep, Standby, Book and Edition also arm a wake for inkharness's `check_at` (a schedule that wakes the board, 5 minutes apart at the soonest); `apps::agentWake()` runs those check-ins right after `standbyWake()`, and powers off again unless PWR is held. No check-ins below 15 %. On USB, PWR doesn't power off: it shows the desk face (the Clock with its weather strip), or the Agent face when it's wanted, and the board stays on to keep it current (`apps::powerKey()`). The RTC alarm is armed only on the way to a power-off and disarmed first thing at boot: while it's fired, INT holds PWRON low, and 4 s of that forces a running board off.
- ESP32 deep sleep also works, but the rails stay up, so it draws more power. Use it only when you need fast wake or wake on a GPIO.
- **Battery:** a 1500 mAh Li-ion cell. Our firmware charges at 500 mA to 4.2 V: 50 mA precharge, stop at 25 mA. Waveshare charges at 200 mA. The input limit is 1500 mA and the power-down threshold 2.6 V (chip defaults, same as Waveshare). A 10 k NTC is fitted on TS. The chip's default (`0x50`) keeps TS out of charge control, and XPowersLib also disables TS measurement. At its default, CHGLED drives charge status onto GPIO2 (`PMIC_CHG`). Waveshare turns it off; we leave it on.

## Driver notes

- **E-paper:** the framebuffer is 48,000 bytes at 1 bpp (96,000 for 4-grey); put it in PSRAM. Wait on BUSY rather than using fixed delays. Always deep-sleep the panel after an update. Partial refreshes accumulate ghosting, so do a full refresh periodically. Waveshare's `EPD_Sleep()` also cuts ALDO3, which wipes panel RAM. After that, a partial refresh needs a re-init plus a re-send of the previous frame as the base. Our `Epd::show()` does both on every update, so it also ends every update by cutting ALDO3 (driving the panel's pins low first), on USB and on battery alike, and the next `show()` powers it again 10 ms before its reset: the panel's supply is on only while it updates.
- **Audio:** the vendor lets `esp_codec_dev` own `AMP_EN`. It stays HIGH from codec open until close, and silence is a DAC mute. We switch the amp, I2S, the codec (into its 0 µA power-down, `es8311::powerDown()`) and ALDO2 per session instead, pause included: a paused player keeps only its open file (`audio.h`). The NS4150B idles at 3 mA and the running codec at about 8 mA. The codec is configured over I2C; audio flows over I2S. VOL 100 is the DAC at 0 dB (`es8311::setVolume()` never adds gain), which leaves speech quiet: the assistant's voices arrive at about −19 dBFS RMS (measured 2026-11-21). So streams (voice replies, `audio::streamStart()`) get +10 dB in software through a peak limiter at −1 dBFS (`streamStep()`); files, the alarm and beeps don't.
- **I2C:** all 5 devices share one bus. Create one bus instance and pass it around; don't let each driver initialise its own.
- **Wi-Fi and BLE scans** (Sensors, `src/wifi_survey.h`, `src/ble_survey.h`): a scan holds the Wi-Fi radio on without joining through `wlan::holdRadio()`, so it can't switch off a connection another task has. BLE is NimBLE straight from the core's S3 libraries (no Arduino BLE library), started for the scan and stopped 4 s after the last screen using it; both survey tasks end then too. It needs `bleInUse()` overridden (`ble_survey.cpp`): without it the core releases the controller's memory at boot and BLE can't start until the next one. Measured 2026-11-19: keeping that memory costs about 26 KB of internal RAM from boot (192 KB free after boot instead of 218 KB), the running stack takes about 45 KB more, which all comes back when it stops, and BLE adds about 230 KB of flash (the Wi-Fi scan screens about 35 KB). Wi-Fi and BLE running at once leave about 68 KB of internal RAM free. So BLE is off by default: build with `INKDECK_BLE=1` (`src/features.h`) for it. The first use of Wi-Fi after a boot, by anything, keeps about 18 KB of internal RAM for good (the system's network tasks); that isn't a leak.
- **Tailnet** (`src/tailnet.h`, `lib/microlink/`): Tailscale on demand, the way `wlan` does Wi-Fi: `tailnet::hold()` joins Wi-Fi and the tailnet, `release()` lets go, and the board leaves a minute after the last user. MicroLink adds a WireGuard interface to lwIP for 100.64.0.0/10, so plain sockets reach tailnet machines once `tailnet::reach()` has woken the peer's session (a connect before that loses its SYN), and listening sockets answer on the tailnet too. Names: `tailnet.cpp` overrides lwIP's weak `lwip_hook_netconn_external_resolve()`, so every `getaddrinfo()` (libsmb2, the NFS client, `esp_http_client`, the agent client) gets tailnet machines from MicroLink's peer list while the board is on it; check the `.elf` with `nm` that it stays a strong `T` (gotcha 11). Arduino's `WiFi.hostByName()` goes through `lwip_getaddrinfo()` in this core, so it sees them too. **LAN first:** the agent client and shares call `tailnet::connectLan()` before holding the tailnet. It tries, in parallel for 1 s, the address the LAN's own DNS gives the name (the hook stays out of that lookup: it skips the task in `lanLookupTask`) and the private endpoints the machine last gave the tailnet (NVS "tailnet"/"lan", ranked: the board's /16, 192.168, 10, then 172.16/12, where Docker bridges sit). Those endpoints alone weren't enough: MicroLink keeps 8 IPv4 endpoints a peer, and a Docker host's bridges fill them before its LAN address (found 2026-11-21); the router's DNS knew the bare name. A share that answers on the LAN mounts by that address. Users: the agent client and shares hold it when their host `routes()` there, `net::Get` for a tailnet URL, Wi-Fi Drive and the updater `join()` it in the background so the LAN never waits, and `tailnet::begin()`'s watcher holds it whenever the board is on USB power (`pmic::status().vbus`, polled every 5 s; a failed join waits a minute, a refused key 15). On USB that leaves about 93 KB of internal RAM free (largest block 49 KB, measured 2026-11-21) instead of about 190 KB. The auth key is in NVS (`settings::tailscaleKey()`), MicroLink's own keys too (namespace `microlink`); a new auth key erases those first, so the board joins as a new machine. Measured 2026-11-21: about 123 KB of flash and 7 KB of internal RAM from boot (MicroLink's static tables), 42 KB of internal RAM for its four task stacks while on the tailnet (their sizes, not measured), and a 512 KB PSRAM buffer while it fetches the map of machines. **Relay region:** MicroLink keeps one DERP connection, to its home region, and relays to every peer through it, so a peer homed in another region never hears a relayed packet and no session comes up until a direct path does (found 2026-11-21: the board on upstream's default 9, Dallas, the agent host on 12, Chicago: `no session ... after 8000 ms`). So the region is a variable (`port/microlink_kconfig.h`), and `settleRegion()` moves the board at its first join of a boot to the agent host's region, else the one most peers use, joining again once and keeping it in NVS ("tailnet"/"derp"). MicroLink's instance keeps its home region across stop and start, hence `ml_inkdeck_forget_home_region()`. Its own logs are compiled out (the core's `CONFIG_LOG_MAXIMUM_LEVEL` is 1) and go to UART0 anyway: for them, build it with `-DLOG_LOCAL_LEVEL=3` in `library.json`, `esp_log_set_vprintf()` to `Serial` and `esp_log_level_set()` its tags (`ml_coord`, `ml_wg_mgr`, `ml_derp`...) to INFO. Tested 2026-11-21: voice to inkharness over the tailnet (join 3.9 s after Wi-Fi, session 0.2 s, 88 KB of internal RAM free with the mic capturing), and by name on the LAN (`onionserve` answered at its LAN address in 35 ms, no join); shares, feeds, Wi-Fi Drive and OTA over it not yet. **Exit node:** `tailscale_exit_node = on` (`settings::tailscaleExitNode()`, read once per boot, since `create()` makes the instance for the boot) passes `advertise_routes = "0.0.0.0/0"`; MicroLink adds `::/0` (Tailscale wants both), enables lwIP NAPT on its WireGuard netif and lets the packet filter (the tailnet's access rules) pass forwarded destinations. It needs `CONFIG_ML_SUBNET_ROUTER` in `port/microlink_kconfig.h`, whose `select`s (`LWIP_IP_FORWARD`, `LWIP_IPV4_NAPT`) the core's prebuilt lwIP already has; IPv6 forwarding it lacks, so IPv6 into the tunnel is dropped (`CONFIG_ML_ROUTER_IPV6` off). If NAPT fails, MicroLink zeroes the routes for the instance's life; `ml_inkdeck_exit_node()` reports what's left, for System's row. On USB the watcher keeps Wi-Fi modem sleep off while the board is an exit node, turning it off again within 5 s when another feature's `WiFi.setSleep(true)` turns it on. A change to `port/microlink_kconfig.h` needs MicroLink's objects deleted (`.pio/build/epaper397/lib*/microlink`): SCons doesn't track a force-included header. Tested 2026-11-21: advertised and NAPT up on USB, internal RAM unchanged (93 KB free); traffic through it not yet.
- **Agent** (`src/agent_client.h`, issue #52): the connection to inkharness is live while an Agent screen is used (two minutes without a key on battery lets it go) or the board is on USB; otherwise polls join, sync and leave (5, 15, 30 minutes, sooner at `check_at`). Background connects (polls, USB, RTC check-ins) are quiet: they don't touch the agent sheet's state or texts, and a talk press takes over. The last list's summary is kept in NVS (`agent`) for Home, `?n`, the chirps and the face; titles only when the PIN doesn't guard the Agent (or `agent_face_details = on`). The face's own record is in `agentface`. Measured 2026-11-21: on USB with the tailnet and the live connection, 90 KB of internal RAM free (largest block 45 KB); the image is 2.35 MB (2.24 MB with `INKDECK_AGENT=0`). Dev key `a` runs the face's off path with one RTC check-in a minute later, and the board then stays on.
- **Comms** (`src/comms.h`, `docs/comms-protocol.md`, issue #66): boards talk to each other over ESP-NOW broadcasts, no router: push-to-talk voice in groups and a flooding mesh. The screens are `src/apps/comms_ui.h` (Home's COMMS at 05; the talk screen, its menu, the Groups and Messages sheets in `comms_ui.cpp`; the dialer's ringing sheet in `comms_ringing.cpp`; the call, ring and conversation sheets that rise over any screen, `apps::pollComms()` that raises them, and the status bar's speech bubble, in `comms_sheet.cpp`). HOLD BOOT on them talks to the boards, not the agent: their `talkTo()` says `comms` with the group and the board (`ui::TalkTo`, with `message` on the ringing sheet and `answer` on the ring sheet; the talk screen calls `nav::talkChanged()` when its cursor moves to another board or it joins a conversation), and `apps/hold_boot.cpp` starts the call from the input timer (`apps::commsTalk()`), before the player sheet. Groups are `mesh_group` lines in `inkdeck.ini` (up to 4; without one, a board with an `agent_token` is in a HOME group made from the token under another HKDF salt), each a secret that gives the group's AES-256-GCM key, its 4-byte id and a rendezvous id (HKDF-SHA256, one label each). Every frame is at most 250 bytes (ESP-NOW v1); a voice frame is 217: 20 ms of IMA ADPCM at 16 kHz, decodable on its own, and its conversation. The nonce is sender id, boot number (a counter in NVS beside the id, `comms`/`boot`) and packet counter, so it never repeats; a 64-packet replay window per sender drops copies, and a seen-cache of 256 drops the mesh's duplicates and decides what to relay (ttl 3; RELAY FOR OTHERS, NVS, on by default). ESP-NOW needs Wi-Fi started but no network: comms holds the radio with `wlan::holdRadio()` and sends on the channel the radio is on, the network's while joined, else `mesh_channel`, which it sets only when `wlan::joining()` is false and there's no connection. Receiving needs modem sleep off: comms turns it off when it starts and again within 250 ms whenever another feature's `WiFi.setSleep(true)` turns it on, and turns it back on when it stops (the tailnet's exit node turns it off again within 5 s). It listens on USB power (`pmic::status().vbus`, polled every 5 s), while `hold()` is held (the Comms screens) or a call is on, and for 10 s after; not at all without a group. One task ("comms", 7 KB stack in internal RAM: writing a message to the card, with FatFs's long names on the stack, peaked at about 4 KB) does everything: the ESP-NOW receive callback (Wi-Fi task) only copies frames into a 96-deep queue in PSRAM; the board's state, the jitter buffer, the call log, the last call (up to 60 s of ADPCM) and messages coming, going or kept are in PSRAM. It uses audio's capture and stream paths like the agent does, so the two take turns: a call doesn't start over the agent's capture or stream, the Recorder or a ringing alarm. **Conversations, rings, messages** (phase 3; `src/comms_calls.h` is their plain state, which `!commstest` drives with a made-up clock): a group has one conversation at a time (a random id; two that meet become the lower), on from an answered ring or the first voice until 60 s after the last word or until nobody is in it. Voice carries its id, and plays only on the boards in it: those that answered, joined, talked, or played the call that opened it (the walkie-talkie: HOLD BOOT with none on opens one, and every listening board that plays it is in), and a board a call is to alone; `leave()` stops it playing there until `join()`, a call of it that plays included. Members say so in their presence every 4 s (with when it began, its quiet and who else), so every board sees it on (`conversation()`), and one not in it counts it once (`status().conversationsSeen`, for a "join?" sheet). `talkStart()` joins the group's conversation (or opens one) and talks in it. `ringStart()` rings all of a group or one board, a packet a second, until an answer (it floods), the dialer gives up, or 60 s (NO ANSWER, which `dial()` keeps until `ringStop()`); each board rung that isn't in the conversation rings (`incomingRing()`, `status().ringsSeen`, `audio::ringChirp()` every 3 s, over music too, which holds for the trill; not over the Recorder, an alarm, the agent or a call), answers each ring packet so the dialer counts it, and stops 5 s after the last ring packet. From 5 s into an unanswered ring (or after NO ANSWER) `messageStart()` stops the ring (a ring end: the others stop ringing) and records up to 60 s, each frame numbered and sent as recorded; the receivers ask again with a bitmap of what they missed until every frame is in and the FNV-1a checksum matches (the sender keeps it 5 minutes; 6 asks in a row with nothing new, or 3 minutes, and a board keeps what it has, marked not complete). Messages are kept on the card in `SD:/.inkdeck/comms/` (`messages.tsv` and `<sender>-<message>.adp`, `src/comms_store.h`), in PSRAM without one (1.5 MB at most), until played and for a day after, 12 at most (the oldest played goes first); they appear in `calls()` (`Kind::Message`) and `messages()`, count as unheard until played (`markHeard()` leaves them), chirp once (`audio::messageChirp()`) and never play by themselves (`playMessage()`). **Baby monitor** (phase 5, the engine; `src/comms_monitor.h` is its plain state and packets, which `!commstest` drives with a made-up clock): `monitorStart(group, listeners, threshold)` on the board left in the room, and only there (no packet starts it, nothing a listener sends opens a mic), for all of the group or up to 8 boards; `monitorStop()` sends a clear off; nothing survives a restart. Packet type 15 was the last 4-bit number, so it's an extended type whose body carries a second type byte: the monitor's six (audio, still-here, off, listening, history ask, history), and later features' after them. The room's mic captures all the time through audio's capture path; each 20 ms frame's level (its loudest sample) against the threshold (−60 to 0 dBFS in 3 dB steps, −30 at first, NVS `comms`/`monthr`) drives `MonitorGate`: every frame is encoded and kept 0.5 s, and goes out 0.5 s after it was captured if a frame at or over the threshold came within 0.5 s after it or 2 s before it (the pre-roll and the hang, always half a second late), as voice's ADPCM in a monitor-audio packet. A still-here every 2 s (threshold, the loudest frame since the last, power, the 2-minute bar now, who may listen), 60 bars of 2 minutes sent to a listener that asks, presence's `kFlagRoom`. The room stays silent: it doesn't ring (nor send ringing), chirp, or play calls to all or a conversation's, and refuses talk, rings, messages, PLAY LAST; it plays only a call to it alone from a board it allows (talk back), its mic stopped for it and back 0.4 s after the speaker has played it out, and that call doesn't raise the call sheet there (`status().incoming` stays; `monitor().talkBack`). An alarm, the Recorder, music or the agent taking the mic or the speaker pauses it the same way (`monitor().paused` says why). `monitorRooms()` lists the rooms that let this board in; `listenStart()` listens to one: the sound plays through the stream path with a call's jitter buffer; a call, PLAY LAST, a message, this board's talk and a ring's trill take the speaker and the monitor plays on with its next frame; SOUND, QUIET 0.6 s after the last frame, LOUD at 12 dB over the room's threshold (one chirp, `audio::monitorChirp()`, at half volume at least when the volume is 30 or below), NO LINK after 20 s without a packet from the room, with an alarm (`audio::monitorAlarm()`, half volume at least) every 2.5 s until `listenAck()` or the link, STOPPED on the room's off. Monitor audio isn't a call: not in `calls()`, not unheard, `status().incoming` unchanged. While the board is a room or listens, comms holds the radio on battery too, `comms::monitorActive()` keeps power.cpp's idle power-off away (`working()`), and it doesn't nap (the radio is held); PWR's power-off sends the room's off (or the listener's "stopped") first (`forceOff()`). Who may listen is an address filter: the group's key seals every packet, so a monitor wants a group whose secret only its listeners have. The mic's start-up thump (3 frames of zeros, then to −39 dBFS over 4 more, measured 2026-11-23) is dropped: 10 frames after every start of the room's mic. - **Memory:** C++ `new` (so `std::string`, `std::vector`, `std::map`) prefers PSRAM (`src/psram_new.cpp`), because IDF's `malloc()` keeps everything under 4 KB in the ~230 KB of internal RAM, and Wi-Fi needs internal RAM to start. Buffers for DMA, task stacks and anything an IRAM interrupt touches must come from `heap_caps_malloc(..., MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL)` (or `MALLOC_CAP_DMA`). TLS buffers go to PSRAM through `net::begin()`. Dev key `h` prints the heap.

## Flashing & recovery

- **If the port is there but nothing answers** (no boot log, no reply to `h`, esptool "No serial data received"): run `uv run tools/board_usb.py check /dev/cu.usbmodem101`, or `... reset` after esptool. A chip reset sometimes leaves the S3's USB-Serial-JTAG controller deaf to the bus while the firmware runs on: about 1 reset in 25-40 in tests on 2026-11-15, for port-open resets and software resets (`esp_restart()`) alike, always right after the ROM hands over to the bootloader. Nothing the host sends gets through, not even control requests (the kernel logs `transaction error` on the board's endpoints, and esptool and JTAG fail too), so no keepalive or chip reset helps. A USB re-enumeration (what a replug does, without cutting power; `board_usb.py` asks the OS for one) brings it back in under a second, with the firmware still running. A replug works too. PWR held 4 s works only if it really cuts power (it didn't once on USB with the battery in). Don't ask the user to go to the board for this. `tools/screenshot.py` checks and re-enumerates by itself.
- If the USB port disappears (crashed firmware, board powered off): **hold BOOT, power-cycle (replug USB, or PWR key on battery), release BOOT** to enter ROM download mode, then upload.
- On battery, hung firmware keeps the board on. Hold PWR for 4 s or more to force it off.
- Stock firmware: 16 MB full-flash images in the upstream repo under `Firmware/Factory procedure/*.bin`. Write them at `0x0` with esptool.
- **Over Wi-Fi** (`src/ota.h`): System → UPDATE OVER WI-FI listens for 10 minutes, then `pio run -t upload --upload-port inkdeck.local` (or the IP shown) pushes the build with espota. It needs `OTA_PASSWORD` in `.env`, the same one the board was built with. The image goes to the idle slot, and only a verified image switches `otadata`, so an interrupted transfer is harmless. Rollback is on: the core's `verifyRollbackLater()` is overridden, and `ota::confirmBoot()` at the end of `setup()` keeps a new image. A crash or reset before that point boots the previous slot. Keep `confirmBoot()` after anything a bad build could break. The boot after an update is a full one (software reset, never an RTC wake), so `apps::standbyWake()`'s power-off before it never meets a pending image. A USB upload rewrites `otadata` (`boot_app0.bin`) and boots `app0`. Images must fit the 3 MB slot (`firmware.bin` is about 2.62 MB now, 2.46 MB with `INKDECK_COMMS=0`, about 0.1 MB less again with `INKDECK_AGENT=0`, and BLE adds about 230 KB; PlatformIO's "Flash used" leaves out the 5 KB `.eh_frame`, so check the `.bin`). The partition table can't change over the air.
- **Wi-Fi drive** (System → WI-FI DRIVE, `src/webdav.h`): WebDAV on port 80 at `inkdeck.local` while its screen is open, signed in as `inkdeck` with the password on the screen (made up once, kept in NVS). From the Mac: `curl --digest -u inkdeck:PASSWORD -X PROPFIND -H "Depth: 1" http://IP/`. `mount_webdav` won't take credentials in the URL and otherwise looks in the Keychain, so drive `mount_webdav -i` with `expect`, and leave out `-S` (no UI): it skips the free-space request that Finder makes, and that request once hid a 90 s hang in macOS's mount (`webdav.h`). Mac mounts show 0 free space; that's on purpose. Requests to the board's IP need the Bash sandbox off. The PIN wheels guard the screen in a `files` build: test with a build that sets `INKDECK_PIN_ON=` (empty) rather than asking for the PIN.
- **Opening the serial port can reset the board.** The USB-Serial-JTAG resets the chip while RTS is set and DTR clear (the signal esptool uses). The OS raises both lines on open and pyserial clears DTR first, so even `dtr = rts = False` before `open()` passes through a reset (`rst:0x15 (USB_UART_CHIP_RESET)`, every open on macOS). Clearing RTS before DTR doesn't: verified 2026-11-15. Open the console with `board_usb.open_console()` (it also does a deliberate reset with `reset=True`), and keep resets few, since each one risks the dead console above. `tools/screenshot.py` resets first by default so `--keys` start from a fresh Home; `--no-reset` drives whatever is on screen. On macOS the port is `/dev/cu.usbmodem*` and PlatformIO is `~/.platformio/penv/bin/pio`; pass `--upload-port`, or it may pick the Bluetooth port.

## Gotchas

1. The wiki tutorials say they use the "ESP32-S3-Zero". That's boilerplate; their pins are for this board.
2. Vendor code defines the IMU interrupt (INT1) as GPIO39: IDF `DOF_INT1`, Arduino `SENSOR_IRQ`. GPIO39 is really the amp enable, and INT1 isn't connected to it (R9 not fitted). Use `IMU_INT2` (GPIO40).
3. The vendor IMU driver has scaling and axis bugs (gyro reads 2× high, gyro X/Y swapped). Check `references/sensors.md` in the skill before reusing it.
4. An RTC alarm set in the past fires only when that date-time comes round next month. Always compute the next wake time from the current RTC time.
5. If PSRAM reports 0 KB, the board's `memory_type` isn't `qio_opi`.
6. On the S3, Arduino's `Serial1` defaults to GPIO15/16, which are SD D0/CLK. Always pass explicit pins.
7. **Never `fwrite()` a sector or more straight from flash to the SD card.** The SDMMC driver DMAs whole 512-byte sectors directly from the caller's buffer, and from a flash address (string constants, `PROGMEM`) the card gets zeros. Verified with dev command `!sdtest`: 1 KB from flash in one call came back all wrong, while internal RAM and PSRAM were exact. Smaller writes go through stdio's buffer and are fine. Copy into a `std::string` first.
8. A quoted `#include "x.h"` looks in the including file's folder first, so a `src/x.h` is shadowed by `src/apps/x.h` for files in `apps/`. Hence `forecast.h` (not `weather.h`) and `podcast_library.h` (not `podcasts.h`) next to `apps/weather.h` and `apps/podcasts.h`.
9. **Keep `-Os` in `build_flags`.** The framework's PlatformIO build script (`tools/platformio-build.py`) loads Espressif's flags files, which leave the optimization level to the Arduino IDE's `platform.txt`, and adds none itself. Without `-Os`, GCC compiles our code, the Arduino core and every library at `-O0`. Only the prebuilt ESP-IDF libraries were optimized. Measured 2026-11-14: the image went from 2.81 MB to 2.08 MB, and standard-library templates instantiated in our code from 279 KB to 34 KB.
10. **The firmware builds with `-fno-exceptions`** (another 110 KB). `throw`, `try` and `catch` don't compile. A library call that throws (`std::stoi` on bad input, `vector::at` out of range, a failed `new`) ends in `abort()`, as it did before when nothing caught it. Check inputs instead. The other size flags in `platformio.ini` (`ESP32_ARDUINO_NO_RGB_BUILTIN`, PNGdec's `NO_GZIP`/`NOBYFOUR`) are explained there. In code, prefer `siscanf` over `sscanf` when no float is parsed, and float math (`sinf`…) over double where precision allows: the float-capable scanner and the double libm cost about 10 KB each.
11. **`src/` is built with LTO** (about 20 KB). `build_src_flags = -flto` covers only our code, and `scripts/lto.py` turns it on at link time. LTO over the Arduino core fails to link (its `__wrap_esp_panic_handler` ends in a "dangerous relocation"), and over libsmb2 saves under 3 KB more. Weak overrides (`verifyRollbackLater()`, `operator new`) and `IRAM_ATTR` survive it; check the `.elf` with `nm` when adding another. The map file names the LTO output `ccXXXX.ltransN.ltrans.o` instead of our `.cpp.o` files.
12. **Hand fonts to `setFont()` through `ui::fonts::drawable()`** (`src/ui/packed_fonts.h`). Fonts of 38 px and up keep their bitmaps deflated in flash (55 KB saved) and have a null `bitmap`. `drawable()` inflates one into PSRAM on first use and returns a font that draws. `ui::drawText()`, `gfx::drawChar()` and the drop cap already do. Measuring works on the font as it is. A packed font set straight on a canvas crashes on the first glyph.
