# AGENTS.md

Firmware for **one device: the Waveshare ESP32-S3-ePaper-3.97**, an ESP32-S3 board with a 3.97"
800×480 B/W e-paper display, PMIC + Li-ion charging, audio, RTC and sensors. Base every pin, driver
and power decision on this file, `include/board_pins.h`, or the primary sources in
[`docs/`](docs/README.md). If they disagree with the schematic, the schematic wins; fix them.

## Hardware

- **MCU:** ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 (240 MHz dual-core, 16 MB flash, 8 MB octal PSRAM, Wi-Fi + BLE 5). USB-C goes directly to the S3's native USB. No UART bridge, no reset button.
- **Display:** SSD1677 controller, 4-wire SPI, write-only. Refresh: full ≈3 s, fast ≈1.5 s, partial ≈0.3 s, 4-grey ≈3 s. Operating range 0–50 °C.
- **PMIC:** TG28, **AXP2101 register-compatible**; Waveshare uses XPowersLib's `XPowersAXP2101`.
- **Peripherals:** ES8311 codec + NS4150B speaker amp + mic, QMI8658A IMU, SHTC3, PCF85063A RTC, microSD (4-bit SDMMC), 3-way rotary switch, BOOT and PWR buttons.
- **Connectors:** MX1.25 for the 3.7 V Li-ion battery, the speaker and an RTC backup cell (marked "reserved"). Test pads on the bottom edge: 5V, GND, 3V3, RX, TX, SDA, SCL, GP0, GP1.

## Power architecture (read before writing sleep/power code)

- **Rails:** DC1 → 3V3 system rail (the ESP32, the amp's VDD, the codec's DVDD/PVDD, sensors, SD). ALDO2 → `Audio_VCC`: the codec's analog supply (AVDD, through R44) **and** the mic. **ALDO3 → the e-paper panel's power switch (Q3 → Q2); if ALDO3 is off, the panel is dead.** While on, that switch draws about 0.3 mA (Q3's base through R29, Q2's gate pull-up R20) even with the panel in deep sleep. ALDO1 (`AudioCTR_VCC`) reaches only R74 (to the amp's CTRL), which isn't fitted, so it has no load. The chip turns on every output at power-up, including DCDC2-4 (no inductors fitted) and ALDO4/BLDO/CPUSLDO/DLDO (unconnected). `pmic::configure()` turns off all but DC1, ALDO2 and ALDO3 at every boot; `audio` switches ALDO2 per session, and the Reader's naps switch ALDO3 off (`pmic.h`).
- **Fixed drain:** T1 sinks R24 (10 k from 3V3 on `PWR_KEY_SENSE`) whenever PWR isn't pressed, so about 0.33 mA flows whenever the board is on. Only a hardware change (a larger R24) helps.
- **On/off:** the PWR key drives the PMIC's `PWRON` pin (Waveshare config: 1 s press = on, 4 s = forced off). The chip's own defaults are 128 ms and 6 s (verified on hardware). Our `pmic::configure()` sets 1 s / 4 s at boot; `references/power.md` §2 has the full comparison. `axp2101.shutdown()` cuts power to everything, including the ESP32.
- **Wake from full power-off:** the RTC's INT output is diode-OR'd onto `PWRON`, so **an RTC alarm boots the board.** The intended low-power loop: update display → panel deep sleep → arm RTC alarm → PMIC shutdown. The e-paper holds its image with no power. The RTC runs from the PMIC's always-on LDO. On boot, `RTC_INT` and `PWR_KEY_SENSE` tell you why the board woke. **Our RTC holds UTC** (unlike the vendor's local time), so alarm registers are UTC; `TIMEZONE` in `.env` gives local time (`src/rtc.h`). `src/power.h` implements the loop: Sleep (wakes for alarms), standby (the Clock face, redrawn by an RTC wake every minute) and Book (a book left open 15 minutes on battery: its cover, off, and the next start opens it again; `src/apps/closed_book.h`). On USB, PWR doesn't power off: it shows the desk face (the Clock with its weather strip) and the board stays on to keep it current (`apps::powerKey()`). The RTC alarm is armed only on the way to a power-off and disarmed first thing at boot: while it's fired, INT holds PWRON low, and 4 s of that forces a running board off.
- ESP32 deep sleep also works, but the rails stay up, so it draws more power. Use it only when you need fast wake or wake on a GPIO.
- **Battery:** a 1500 mAh Li-ion cell. Our firmware charges at 500 mA to 4.2 V: 50 mA precharge, stop at 25 mA. Waveshare charges at 200 mA. The input limit is 1500 mA and the power-down threshold 2.6 V (chip defaults, same as Waveshare). A 10 k NTC is fitted on TS. The chip's default (`0x50`) keeps TS out of charge control, and XPowersLib also disables TS measurement. At its default, CHGLED drives charge status onto GPIO2 (`PMIC_CHG`). Waveshare turns it off; we leave it on.

## Driver notes

- **E-paper:** the framebuffer is 48,000 bytes at 1 bpp (96,000 for 4-grey); put it in PSRAM. Wait on BUSY rather than using fixed delays. Always deep-sleep the panel after an update. Partial refreshes accumulate ghosting, so do a full refresh periodically. Waveshare's `EPD_Sleep()` also cuts ALDO3, which wipes panel RAM. After that, a partial refresh needs a re-init plus a re-send of the previous frame as the base. Our `Epd::show()` does both on every update, so the Reader's naps cut ALDO3 (`Epd::powerOff()`, which drives the panel's pins low first) and the next `show()` powers it again; dev command `!paneloff` tries that path on USB.
- **Audio:** the vendor lets `esp_codec_dev` own `AMP_EN`. It stays HIGH from codec open until close, and silence is a DAC mute. We switch the amp, I2S, the codec (into its 0 µA power-down, `es8311::powerDown()`) and ALDO2 per session instead, pause included: a paused player keeps only its open file (`audio.h`). The NS4150B idles at 3 mA and the running codec at about 8 mA. The codec is configured over I2C; audio flows over I2S.
- **I2C:** all 5 devices share one bus. Create one bus instance and pass it around; don't let each driver initialise its own.
- **Memory:** C++ `new` (so `std::string`, `std::vector`, `std::map`) prefers PSRAM (`src/psram_new.cpp`), because IDF's `malloc()` keeps everything under 4 KB in the ~230 KB of internal RAM, and Wi-Fi needs internal RAM to start. Buffers for DMA, task stacks and anything an IRAM interrupt touches must come from `heap_caps_malloc(..., MALLOC_CAP_INTERNAL)` (or `MALLOC_CAP_DMA`). TLS buffers go to PSRAM through `net::begin()`. Dev key `h` prints the heap.

## Flashing & recovery

- **If the port is there but nothing answers** (no boot log, no reply to `h`, esptool "No serial data received"): run `uv run tools/board_usb.py check /dev/cu.usbmodem101`, or `... reset` after esptool. A chip reset sometimes leaves the S3's USB-Serial-JTAG controller deaf to the bus while the firmware runs on: about 1 reset in 25-40 in tests on 2026-11-15, for port-open resets and software resets (`esp_restart()`) alike, always right after the ROM hands over to the bootloader. Nothing the host sends gets through, not even control requests (the kernel logs `transaction error` on the board's endpoints, and esptool and JTAG fail too), so no keepalive or chip reset helps. A USB re-enumeration (what a replug does, without cutting power; `board_usb.py` asks the OS for one) brings it back in under a second, with the firmware still running. A replug works too. PWR held 4 s works only if it really cuts power (it didn't once on USB with the battery in). Don't ask the user to go to the board for this. `tools/screenshot.py` checks and re-enumerates by itself.
- If the USB port disappears (crashed firmware, board powered off): **hold BOOT, power-cycle (replug USB, or PWR key on battery), release BOOT** to enter ROM download mode, then upload.
- On battery, hung firmware keeps the board on. Hold PWR for 4 s or more to force it off.
- Stock firmware: 16 MB full-flash images in the upstream repo under `Firmware/Factory procedure/*.bin`. Write them at `0x0` with esptool.
- **Over Wi-Fi** (`src/ota.h`): System → UPDATE OVER WI-FI listens for 10 minutes, then `pio run -t upload --upload-port inkdeck.local` (or the IP shown) pushes the build with espota. It needs `OTA_PASSWORD` in `.env`, the same one the board was built with. The image goes to the idle slot, and only a verified image switches `otadata`, so an interrupted transfer is harmless. Rollback is on: the core's `verifyRollbackLater()` is overridden, and `ota::confirmBoot()` at the end of `setup()` keeps a new image. A crash or reset before that point boots the previous slot. Keep `confirmBoot()` after anything a bad build could break. The boot after an update is a full one (software reset, never an RTC wake), so `apps::standbyWake()`'s power-off before it never meets a pending image. A USB upload rewrites `otadata` (`boot_app0.bin`) and boots `app0`. Images must fit the 3 MB slot (`firmware.bin` is about 1.97 MB now; PlatformIO's "Flash used" leaves out the 5 KB `.eh_frame`, so check the `.bin`). The partition table can't change over the air.
- **Wi-Fi drive** (System → WI-FI DRIVE, `src/webdav.h`): WebDAV on port 80 at `inkdeck.local` while its screen is open, signed in as `inkdeck` with the password on the screen (made up once, kept in NVS). From the Mac: `curl --digest -u inkdeck:PASSWORD -X PROPFIND -H "Depth: 1" http://IP/`. `mount_webdav` won't take credentials in the URL and otherwise looks in the Keychain, so drive `mount_webdav -i` with `expect`, and leave out `-S` (no UI): it skips the free-space request that Finder makes, and that request once hid a 90 s hang in macOS's mount (`webdav.h`). Mac mounts show 0 free space; that's on purpose. Requests to the board's IP need the Bash sandbox off. The PIN wheels guard the screen in a `files` build: test with a build that sets `INKDECK_PIN_ON=` (empty) rather than asking for the PIN.
- **Opening the serial port can reset the board.** The USB-Serial-JTAG resets the chip while RTS is set and DTR clear (the signal esptool uses). The OS raises both lines on open and pyserial clears DTR first, so even `dtr = rts = False` before `open()` passes through a reset (`rst:0x15 (USB_UART_CHIP_RESET)`, every open on macOS). Clearing RTS before DTR doesn't: verified 2026-11-15. Open the console with `board_usb.open_console()` (it also does a deliberate reset with `reset=True`), and keep resets few, since each one risks the dead console above. `tools/screenshot.py` resets first by default so `--keys` start from a fresh Home; `--no-reset` drives whatever is on screen. On macOS the port is `/dev/cu.usbmodem*` and PlatformIO is `~/.platformio/penv/bin/pio`; pass `--upload-port`, or it may pick the Bluetooth port.

## Gotchas

1. The wiki tutorials say they use the "ESP32-S3-Zero". That's boilerplate; their pins are for this board.
2. Vendor code defines the IMU interrupt (INT1) as GPIO39: IDF `DOF_INT1`, Arduino `SENSOR_IRQ`. GPIO39 is really the amp enable, and INT1 isn't connected to it (R9 not fitted). Use `IMU_INT2` (GPIO40).
3. The vendor IMU driver has scaling and axis bugs (gyro reads 2× high, gyro X/Y swapped). Check `references/sensors.md` in the skill before reusing it.
4. An RTC alarm set in the past fires only when that date-time comes round next month. Always compute the next wake time from the current RTC time.
5. If PSRAM reports 0 KB, the board's `memory_type` isn't `qio_opi`.
6. On the S3, Arduino's `Serial1` defaults to GPIO15/16, which are SD D0/CLK. Always pass explicit pins.
7. **Never `fwrite()` a sector or more straight from flash to the SD card.** The SDMMC driver DMAs whole 512-byte sectors directly from the caller's buffer, and from a flash address (string constants, `PROGMEM`) the card gets zeros. Verified with dev command `!sdtest`: 1 KB from flash in one call came back all wrong, while internal RAM and PSRAM were exact. Smaller writes go through stdio's buffer and are fine. Copy into a `std::string` first.
8. A quoted `#include "x.h"` looks in the including file's folder first, so a `src/x.h` is shadowed by `src/apps/x.h` for files in `apps/`. Hence `forecast.h` (not `weather.h`) and `podcast_library.h` (not `podcasts.h`) next to `apps/weather.h` and `apps/podcasts.h`.
9. **Keep `-Os` in `build_flags`.** The framework's PlatformIO build script (`tools/platformio-build.py`) loads Espressif's flags files, which leave the optimization level to the Arduino IDE's `platform.txt`, and adds none itself. Without `-Os`, GCC compiles our code, the Arduino core and every library at `-O0`. Only the prebuilt ESP-IDF libraries were optimized. Measured 2026-11-14: the image went from 2.81 MB to 2.08 MB, and standard-library templates instantiated in our code from 279 KB to 34 KB.
10. **The firmware builds with `-fno-exceptions`** (another 110 KB). `throw`, `try` and `catch` don't compile. A library call that throws (`std::stoi` on bad input, `vector::at` out of range, a failed `new`) ends in `abort()`, as it did before when nothing caught it. Check inputs instead. The other size flags in `platformio.ini` (`ESP32_ARDUINO_NO_RGB_BUILTIN`, PNGdec's `NO_GZIP`/`NOBYFOUR`) are explained there. In code, prefer `siscanf` over `sscanf` when no float is parsed, and float math (`sinf`…) over double where precision allows: the float-capable scanner and the double libm cost about 10 KB each.
11. **`src/` is built with LTO** (about 20 KB). `build_src_flags = -flto` covers only our code, and `scripts/lto.py` turns it on at link time. LTO over the Arduino core fails to link (its `__wrap_esp_panic_handler` ends in a "dangerous relocation"), and over libsmb2 saves under 3 KB more. Weak overrides (`verifyRollbackLater()`, `operator new`) and `IRAM_ATTR` survive it; check the `.elf` with `nm` when adding another. The map file names the LTO output `ccXXXX.ltransN.ltrans.o` instead of our `.cpp.o` files.
12. **Hand fonts to `setFont()` through `ui::fonts::drawable()`** (`src/ui/packed_fonts.h`). Fonts of 38 px and up keep their bitmaps deflated in flash (55 KB saved) and have a null `bitmap`. `drawable()` inflates one into PSRAM on first use and returns a font that draws. `ui::drawText()`, `gfx::drawChar()` and the drop cap already do. Measuring works on the font as it is. A packed font set straight on a canvas crashes on the first glyph.
